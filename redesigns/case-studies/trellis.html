Context

Two ways to shop for car insurance

Savvy.Insure, by Trellis, helps people find better car insurance. Customers could shop in one of two ways.

  • Linked. Sign in to your current insurer, say Progressive, and we pull your details from that account to find comparable quotes.
  • Unlinked. For anyone without insurance, or who couldn’t sign in: a series of questions asking for the minimum we needed to quote them.

Both start on the homepage, and every step from there to a quote is where the business wins or loses.

Opportunity

No designer for two years

Savvy hadn’t had a designer for two years, and the CEO believed there were many chances to lift conversion across the main car insurance flows.

So the opportunity wasn’t one problem but many: the homepage, the linked flow and the unlinked flow. The job was to find the biggest wins quickly, and prove them.

Approach

Fifteen experiments in three months

  • The cheapest way to learn. Not every experiment needs to be an A/B test. We framed each change as a hypothesis, picked the lightest method that could confirm or disconfirm it, and used A/B tests where we needed statistical confidence.
  • One small team, a steady cadence. With a front-end engineer, a back-end engineer and a growth engineering consultant, I designed and shipped around 15 experiments in three months, across the homepage, the linked flow and the unlinked flow.
  • Making it a habit. I co-wrote and presented How do we make good decisions about product investment?, sharing how we chose methods, from interviews to A/B tests, and why learning matters more than being right.

One example: ask for the car before the phone number

From 17 to 31 May 2023, 44,846 people started the unlinked application and 26,333 finished it: 58.7%. Four in ten gave up before seeing a quote.

Hypothesis: changing the order of the unlinked application will increase conversion.

The control opened by asking for name, phone, email and date of birth, before Savvy had given anything back. V2 starts with the car and the drivers, builds up to the policy, and only asks for contact details at the very end, as “Where should we send your offers?”

Also: a new homepage

I also redesigned the homepage, where both paths begin, to make the value clear up front: better insurers, real savings, and help at no cost.

Outcome

$3.5MM in predicted annual revenue

  • Across around 15 experiments in three months, the wins added up to $3.5MM in predicted gross annual revenue.
  • The re-order test alone lifted completion of the unlinked application from 60.4% to 69.0%, a 14% relative improvement.
  • We left the team with the infrastructure to keep testing with ease, and a shared way of deciding what to build.

What we learned

  • People need Savvy to feel valuable before they give their info. Order matters.
  • Learning is more important than being right. Good testing infrastructure means more people share ideas, and it takes the HiPPO out of decisions.
