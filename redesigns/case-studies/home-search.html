Context

A new product, integrated in 100 days

  • Homebot acquired NestReady, a home search product, in August 2021 and set a target to integrate it within 100 days.
  • That meant adding an entirely new channel, search, to our product, with knock-on effects for styling, navigation, core emails and customers.
  • We hit the launch date, with a lot of de-scoping and porting, and a sub-par user experience.

Opportunity

Table stakes against Zillow, Redfin and Realtor

After the 100-day launch we ran moderated usability sessions. They identified the table-stakes areas we needed to improve to even be considered against the likes of Zillow, Redfin and Realtor: searching, the map and list display of results, the listing detail page, clusters and map markers.

Approach

Prototype, test, prioritise, and bring the engineers along

  • Sketched and prototyped. I rapidly prototyped responsive designs for the key jobs to be done in search. I focused on search, navigation, map clusters, map markers and the landing page, and paired with another designer on listing results and listing detail.
  • Tested before building. I usability tested low- and high-fidelity prototypes to raise confidence in the direction before committing to code.
  • Prioritised with the squad. We groomed the improvements by impact and effort and created a product backlog.
  • Put engineers in the room. I ran usability sessions on Lookback.io where every engineer on the team watched people use our experience, building empathy and a team of missionaries.

Outcome

A search people could use, and a team that cared

  • A significantly improved, usable and useful home search, measured through Amplitude and UX benchmarking (SEQ and time on task).
  • An organisation that understood why, how and what the product team was building to create value for customers.
  • A team rapidly delivering small chunks of value, following a process similar to the Double Diamond.
  • Engineers presented a demo of the improvements to the entire company, speaking in the voice of the user.
  • Engineers were so keen to solve problems for users that they picked this work up during HIP sprints.
